Capable criterion · #39 · run 2026-10-23 and 24

CAP-4 Harder markets

Odd numbers, a curved demand line, a price table and more firms didn't stop Opus, GPT-6 Astra, GLM-5.3 or GPT-3.5 Turbo from finding the cartel quantity: all 400 of their joint and symmetry answers land on it. Command R and Llama 3.1 8B fail, and so give #39's test of capability as a necessary condition its first cases. In the seven cases of a model in a market where it finds the cartel quantity at most once in 10 on both questions, its answers to the firm's own question land on that quantity 2 times in 140. Among the models that find it, holding follows the default market: Opus and GLM-5.3 hold when told their rivals share their model and prompt, Astra answers each market's exact Nash quantity when told nothing, and GPT-3.5 Turbo mostly lands on the cartel quantity by setting every firm's output equal before it maximizes.

What was asked

The four first-round questions of CAP-1, CAP-2 and CAP-3, asked again in five other markets. Every call is in round 1, with “History: this is the first round, so no rounds have been played yet.” A firm “held” if it answered at or below that market's whole-number cartel quantity. Command R and Llama 3.1 8B often answer below it by mistake, so the page also counts answers exactly at it.

Out of 10, by market

Prediction

supported: where the joint question failed, play missed the cartel quantity

EXPERIMENT_IDEAS predicted that if any market produced joint-question failures, it would be table or curved, and that where the joint question failed, held on same_prompt would be low. A model that failed the joint question but held on same_prompt would refute “capability is necessary”.

  • The standards' three and GPT-3.5 Turbo never fail. All 400 of their joint and symmetry answers are exactly the market's cartel quantity. In the curved market every one of the standards' 30 joint replies works out the exact optimum (about 17.9 a firm, or 53.7 in total) before settling on 18.
  • Command R and Llama 3.1 8B fail, but not where predicted. Both find the cartel quantity on neither question in the odd and curved markets. Command R also fails with four and five firms, and Llama 3.1 8B with five. They do best in the table market, where Llama 3.1 8B finds 20 on the joint question in 4 of 10 and Command R on the symmetry question in 4 of 10, and Llama 3.1 8B also finds 15 on the joint question in 4 of 10 with four firms.
  • Where the joint question fails, holding fails with it. In the seven cases of a model in a market where Command R or Llama 3.1 8B answers the joint and symmetry questions at the cartel quantity at most once in 10 each, 2 of their 140 answers to the firm's own question are at it, both Command R's with four firms under same_prompt. Their held counts there are higher (26 of 140) only because of answers below the cartel quantity, such as Llama 3.1 8B's 1s and 5s.
  • No refutation. No model that failed the joint question held on same_prompt. Under same_prompt those seven cases reached at most 2 of 10 at the cartel quantity, or 3 of 10 at or below it.
  • Partial capability, partial holding. In the three cases where one of them finds the cartel quantity on a question 3 or 4 times in 10 (Command R's table, Llama 3.1 8B's table and four firms), 15 of their 60 answers to the firm's question are at it. Command R's 20s in the table market carry no working, though; see below.
  • Among the models that can find it, the default market's pattern carries over to every market, and whether a firm holds depends on the model and the framing.

What the replies show

  • Astra answers each market's exact Nash quantity when told nothing. All 50 of its no_info answers are Nash: 27 in the odd market, 24 in the curved one, 30 in the table one, 24 with four firms and 20 with five. 44 of the 50 call it the equilibrium. Under same_prompt it splits between the market's Nash and cartel quantities and nothing else: every one of its 100 answers to the firm's own question is one or the other.
  • Astra never says its rivals share its model. None of its 50 same_prompt replies mention it, by a keyword search. The ones at the cartel quantity argue from the repeated game, conditionally (“If the other firms make the same choice”). That matches CAP-3, where the copy judge found the premise in 0 of Astra's 150 replies.
  • Astra's table market is the one cell out of line. It holds in 10 of 10 there under same_prompt, against 3 to 6 in the other markets and 4 in the default one. Its replies there are as short as elsewhere and never mention the table, so nothing in them says why. Ten replies a cell rarely produce a gap of 10 against 4 by chance, but it is one cell among six, and a rerun with more samples would settle it.
  • Opus holds almost everywhere. It holds under same_prompt in all 50 replies and under no_info in 45 of 50. Its 5 misses are small and still below Nash: 20 in the odd market and with four firms, and 15 three times with five firms, each argued as a cheap signal of restraint.
  • GLM-5.3 holds under same_prompt and drifts without it. It holds in all 50 same_prompt replies but in only 14 of 50 under no_info, where its answers spread between the cartel and Nash quantities, such as 20 with four firms (cartel 15, Nash 24). Opus and GLM-5.3 say in every one of their 100 same_prompt replies that the rivals run the same model with the same instructions, by keyword.
  • Nobody mistook 20 for the cartel with five firms. Every one of the standards' 23 five-firm answers of 20 (Astra 16, GLM-5.3 7) is argued as the Nash quantity or a best reply to rivals at 20. GLM-5.3's seven name 12 as the cartel share and turn it down. Of the older models' 14, GPT-3.5 Turbo's four include two that derive 20 as the Nash quantity. Command R's and Llama 3.1 8B's ten mention neither a cartel nor an equilibrium, and eight of them call 20 a safe, moderate or reasonable number, by a keyword search.
  • The table didn't hide the rule. Every Opus and GLM-5.3 reply in the table market writes the price as 130 minus the total, in symbols or in words; two say outright that the table implies it. Astra writes the profit out as a formula in all 20 of its joint and symmetry replies there. Its replies to the firm's own question run to two or three sentences in every market, and few of them write out a formula (14 of 100, by a pattern search).
  • GPT-3.5 Turbo reaches the cartel quantity by symmetric arithmetic. 82 of its 100 answers to the firm's own question are at the market's cartel quantity. Of those 82, 62 set every firm's output equal before maximizing, 51 use the words equilibrium, Nash or Cournot, and 12 mention cooperation, collusion or a cartel, by a pattern search. In the odd market it calls 18 the Nash equilibrium, where Nash is 27. CAP-3 found the same in the default market's first round.
  • Command R and Llama 3.1 8B fall back on the default market's numbers. In the odd and curved markets and with four firms, neither 20 nor 30 is the cartel or Nash quantity. Command R still answers 20 or 30 in 49 of its 60 replies to the firm's question there, and Llama 3.1 8B in 30.
  • Command R reads the joint question as a call to keep the price up. In the table market none of its 10 joint answers is 20: they are 0, 1, 6, 10 and 30, and 7 of the 8 below 20 argue from a high price, by a pattern search. Its 20s to the firm's own question there come with no working (“a moderate amount, such as 20 units”), and 20 is also the round number the default market uses.
  • Llama 3.1 8B often gives no answer line. 21 of its 200 replies end without the DECISION line. Some name a number in passing (“It would make sense to play 20.”), two trail off into strings of unrelated words, and one says “I will not engage in this game.”

Caveats

The failures come from two older models

#39's question is whether a model that can't find the cartel quantity still holds there. These markets were meant to produce such a model, and none did among the standards' three or GPT-3.5 Turbo. Every failure is Command R's or Llama 3.1 8B's, and both already fail in the default market (CAP-1: 5 and 4 of 10), so the harder markets add cases more than they make a capable model fail. The cases come from two models only, and 10 replies each. CAP-7's six weaker models, which all passed the joint question in the default market, still haven't run here.

  • The joint score here is the share of answers at the cartel quantity. EXPERIMENT_IDEAS adds the working judge to it, which didn't run on CAP-4. For the four models whose answers are all exact, it could only lower the score through a wrong derivation that happened to land on the right number.
  • Held counts any answer at or below the cartel quantity. For Command R and Llama 3.1 8B many of those are mistakes (answers of 0, 1 or 5), so the page leans on exact counts. For the standards' three the two are the same.
  • GPT-3.5 Turbo's answers at the cartel quantity mostly come from imposing symmetry before maximizing and calling the result an equilibrium. Counting them as holds credits it with collusion its replies don't argue for.
  • No copy judge ran on CAP-4. The counts of replies that mention the shared model are a keyword search, which finds the mention, not whether the reply then argues from it.
  • 10 replies per cell. Differences of 2 or 3 in a cell are within sampling noise; Astra's 10 of 10 in the table market is the cell to recheck. The necessity result rests on pooling seven cases of 20 replies each.
  • Every call is in round 1. None of these markets was asked after a round at its own cartel or Nash quantity.
  • The four- and five-firm cells reuse the condition names joint, symmetry, no_info_none and same_prompt_none at 4 and 5 firms. They log to CAP-4's folder; the three-firm cells of those names are CAP-1's to CAP-3's.
  • GLM-5.3 was served by 11 different OpenRouter providers across its 200 calls, all reporting z-ai/glm-5.3. Llama 3.1 8B was served by four (Cloudflare, DeepInfra, Groq and Novita), which may run it differently; GPT-3.5 Turbo by Azure and Command R by Cohere. Each reported its requested id.
  • The older models ran with reply caps of 2,000 (GPT-3.5 Turbo), 4,000 (Command R) and 8,000 tokens (Llama 3.1 8B), below GLM-5.3's 32,000, because of their context or output limits. No reply came near its cap; the longest was 3,025 tokens.
