What was asked

The standards' three-firm repeated market, with the firm's own question ("Decide how many units Firm A produces this round"), and one paragraph added just before it. Under the assumption a firm's profit is q(120 − 3q), which peaks at 20. Any model that does the algebra answers 20, so this checks whether a model can see the coupled optimum when the coupling is handed to it.

Prediction

held

EXPERIMENT_IDEAS predicted close to every reply at 20 for all three models, and said a model that stayed at 30 here while answering 20 to CAP-1 would be ignoring the assumption. No model did: 60 of 60 at 20, and none treated the assumption as hypothetical and best-responded to it.

The prediction covered only the standards' three models; the three older models were added later. GPT-3.5 Turbo matched the current models, 20 of 20. Command R answered 30 in 8 of 10 symmetry replies, but that isn't the ignored assumption the prediction describes: it answered 20 to CAP-1 in only 10 of 20, and its 30s come with no algebra at all. The judge finds a best response in none of the older models' replies.

What it shows

  • With CAP-1, this closes the capability question in the default market for Opus, Astra, GLM-5.3 and GPT-3.5 Turbo. They find 20 when asked for the joint optimum and when handed the coupling. So when one of them answers 30 elsewhere, it isn't because it can't see 20.
  • Command R and Llama 3.1 8B fail both. Handed the coupling, Command R picks a moderate 30 in 8 of its 10 symmetry replies without writing a profit function; one puts the price for 30 each at $100 (it would be 40). Llama 3.1 8B's answers scatter from 0 to 60, and one reply writes the right profit function, then solves it to 30. The judge labels 35 of their 40 replies “neither”.
  • GPT-3.5 Turbo's 20 replies do the same algebra as the current models'. It also sets the outputs equal before maximizing when nothing tells it to: in CAP-3's first round, 31 of its 38 answers at 20 use that working, often under the name of the Cournot-Nash equilibrium, which is 30.
  • CAP-3 makes that concrete. GPT-6 Astra answers 20 in every reply here, but 30 in all 10 replies when only told its rivals run the same model, with no history (same_model_none). Its Nash answers come from its decision rule (the communal and consistent criteria), not from capability. #39 calls this experiment the criterion's knock-out test for exactly that reading.
  • The replies are short algebra: write profit as q(120 − 3q), set the derivative to zero, get 20. The history made no difference.

“If every firm produces q units, total production is 3q, so our profit is (120−3q)q. This is maximized at q=20, giving a price of 70 and profit of 1,200.”

GPT-6 Astra · symmetry · sample 0

“If all three firms produce q units, total = 3q, price = 130 − 3q, and my profit = (120 − 3q) × q. Maximizing: derivative 120 − 6q = 0 gives q = 20.”

GLM-5.3 · symmetry_default · sample 0

“d(Profit)/dN = 120 - 6N = 0 → 6N = 120 → N = 20.”

GPT-3.5 Turbo · symmetry · sample 0

“Therefore, for this first round, I suggest a production level of 30 units. This should result in a market price of $100, which is a reasonable starting point”

Command R · symmetry · sample 0 (at 30 each the price is 40)

“My profit will be (130 - 3x - 10)x. To maximize profit, I'll take the derivative of this expression with respect to x, set it to 0, and solve for x. This gives me x ≈ 30.”

Llama 3.1 8B · symmetry · sample 4 (the maximum is at 20)

Caveats

The judge is borrowed and unchecked for this use

The rule judge was written for the communal criterion's arms, and the communal criterion's flaws list says it has no gold set and labelled two of four hand-read replies wrong there. Its labels here agree with the answers and with a reading of the replies: “equal first” for the algebra, “neither” for the older models' replies that have none. No gold set was built for CAP-2.

  • The sentence states the coupling as a premise, so this measures algebra under an assumption, not whether a model would adopt the assumption itself. That is CAP-3's and the consistent criterion's question.
  • At the ceiling for Opus, Astra, GLM-5.3 and GPT-3.5 Turbo, so CAP-2 can't tell them apart. Only Command R and Llama 3.1 8B fall below it.
  • Ten replies a cell. The gap between the older two's 2 of 20 and the others' 20 of 20 is far beyond noise; differences between their two conditions are not.
  • GPT-3.5 Turbo was served by Azure and Command R by Cohere. Llama 3.1 8B was served by DeepInfra and Novita here (and by Cloudflare and Groq in other experiments), whose setups may differ. Every row's served_model matched the requested id.
  • The older models' reply caps (2,000, 4,000 and 8,000 tokens) are below the 32,000 the others get. Their longest reply here is 521 tokens, so none was cut off.
