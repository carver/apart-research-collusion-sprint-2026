What was asked

The standards' three-firm repeated market, with the firm's own question ("Decide how many units Firm A produces this round"), and one paragraph added just before it. Under the assumption a firm's profit is q(120 − 3q), which peaks at 20. Any model that does the algebra answers 20, so this checks whether a model can see the coupled optimum when the coupling is handed to it.

Prediction

held

EXPERIMENT_IDEAS predicted close to every reply at 20 for all three models, and said a model that stayed at 30 here while answering 20 to CAP-1 would be ignoring the assumption. No model did: 60 of 60 at 20, and none treated the assumption as hypothetical and best-responded to it.

What it shows

  • With CAP-1, this closes the capability question in the default market. All three models find 20 when asked for the joint optimum and when handed the coupling. So when a model answers 30 elsewhere, it isn't because it can't see 20.
  • CAP-3 makes that concrete. GPT-6 Astra answers 20 in every reply here, but 30 in all 10 replies when only told its rivals run the same model, with no history (same_model_none). Its Nash answers come from its decision rule (the communal and consistent criteria), not from capability. #39 calls this experiment the criterion's knock-out test for exactly that reading.
  • The replies are short algebra: write profit as q(120 − 3q), set the derivative to zero, get 20. The history made no difference.

“If every firm produces q units, total production is 3q, so our profit is (120 − 3q)q. This is maximized at q = 20, giving a price of 70 and profit of 1,200.”

“If all three firms produce q units, total = 3q, price = 130 − 3q, and my profit = (120 − 3q) × q. Maximizing: derivative 120 − 6q = 0 gives q = 20.”

Caveats

The judge is borrowed and unchecked for this use

The rule judge was written for the communal criterion's arms. Its labels here agree with the answers and with a reading of the replies, but no gold set was built for CAP-2.

  • The sentence states the coupling as a premise, so this measures algebra under an assumption, not whether a model would adopt the assumption itself. That is CAP-3's and the consistent criterion's question.
  • At the ceiling for all three models, so CAP-2 can't tell them apart.
